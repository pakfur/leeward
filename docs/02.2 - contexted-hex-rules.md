# Contested Hex Rules
Contested Hex resolution happens during MOVEMENT RESOLUTION PHASE and may involve 
UI for player input.

A contested hex situation occurs when portions of two ships attempt to entering
the same hex (a hex which is either empty or being vacated by another ship) 
during the same turn. The following rules determine which ship moves into the hex, 
and the consequences.

# Simultaneity of Movement. 
Ships move at the same time, and all movement for each turn is completed before 
checking for collisions or contested hex situations.

# Winning Contested Hexes. 
Each ship involved in the contested hex rolls 1-6. 
The ship with the highest adjusted die roll enters the hex. 
If the adjusted dice rolls are the same, the ships roll again, 
until one ship or the other gets a higher dice roll. 

The following modifiers apply:
-  +1 for every Crew Quality level better than the opposing ship.
-  +1 for a ship that has plotted to use more MP’s than the opposing ship.
-  +1 for every class larger than opposing ship.

# Surrendering Contested Hexes. 
If one of the ships has a larger net Die Roll Modifier than the other, 
the user is presented with a UI to elect to automatically 'lose' the contested hex. 
In this case the ship with the lower net Die Roll Modifier would enter the hex.
If both ships have the same Dice Modifier or the user elects to not "lose" the 
contexted hex, then resolution follows the remaining rules.

## Losing Contexted Hexes
The ship that wins the contested hex die roll enters the contested hex; 
the loser must either attempt to bear off or collide with the winner. The ship that 
must bear off will be presented with a "Bear Off" UI choice to make the attempt,
or continue. See Bearing Off Rules for bearing off resolution.

## More Than Two Ships. 
If more than two ships attempt to enter the same hex in the same impulse, 
the same procedure as for two ships is followed, with each contesting ship 
rolling a die, The highest die roll among the ships attempting to enter 
gets the hex. If all ships obtain the same adjusted DR, ail roll again. 
If two or more ships tie for the highest adjusted DR, only they roll again; 
the other ships lose the hex. This process continues until one ship gets the 
hex. The others must try to bear off or collide with the ship that entered the 
hex.


# Bearing Off Rules

## Potential Collision. 
A potential collision occurs when one ship attempts to move forward into a hex 
occupied by another ship. This may occur either because the first ship is 
attempting to enter a hex already occupied by the second ship at the start of 
the current turn, or because the first ship has lost a contested hex roll 
against the second ship. In either of these cases, the first ship may attempt 
to bear off to avoid colliding with the second ship.

## Eligibility and Procedure. 
Determine if a ship can bear off by consulting the bearing_off_table.json. 
If the turning rules determine that a ship is elegible to turn, then the ship
has a percent chance to turn by looking up the crew quality ad ship manuverability 
against the bearing_off_table.json. If the ship suceeds, the ship may turn any eligible
direction. 

## Successfully Bearing Off. 
If successful, the ship turns in the desired direction immediately and ends its 
move. If this would violate deceleration limits, the ship must move a number of 
hexes forward sufficient to fulfill the minimum movement requirements.

## Failure to Bear Off
If a ship chooses to not bear off, or is not eligible to turn, or fails the percent
chance roll from the bearing_off_table.json, then it collides. Consult the Collisions
and Fouling Rules

# Collisions and Fouling Rules
## Stoppage
Both ships involved in the collision stop movement for the remainder of the 
Movement Phase, even if this violates normal deceleration limits.

# Rigging Loss Due to Collision
A ship which collides with another ship and which is pointing at the other 
ship loses 2R if at Fighting Sail, 4R if at Maneuver Sail, and 6R if at 
Plain Sail. (Exception: A ship which collides by drifting into another ship 
does not lose extra rigging.)

## Determining Fouling. 
After subtracting the collision damage, the player has a 50% chance of 
having thier ship fouled with the ship they hit. 

## Dismasted Ships. 
Dismasted ships may never become fouled with other ships. A ship that is 
already fouled, then becomes dismasted, remains fouled. Ships which become 
dismasted as the result of a collision may become fouled during that 
Movement Phase

## Unfouling 
Fouled ships may attempt to unfoul by rolling on the Unfouiing Table during 
the MAINTENANCE PHASE.
