# Close Action vs. Leeward: Feature Diff

**Scope:** *Close Action: The Age of Fighting Sail, Vol. 1* (1997) rules book, sections 1–14 (`docs/rules/close_action.md`, lines 132–1967). The rating system, tournament rules, designer's notes and scenario book are out of scope.

**Compared against:** the Leeward code on `feature/rule-fixes` at `7a5c9dc` (`main` at `30a2c35` plus the rule-fixes sub-project) (`scripts/`, `data/rules/`, `data/scenarios/`). Rows changed later were re-checked against the code when they were edited, in the commit that made the change: `13f8382` blocked wind (5.G), collision rigging loss (6.E.3), the fast-tack bonus removal (5.B.3), next-turn wind changes (3.B.6); `2a2374a` bearing off as a decision (6.D); `e68aa2f` critical hits (7.D.7–12); `ec1085e` the crew-task engine and Automatic crew (crew-tasks spec phase 1: 4.A.3, 4.B, 8.D.1 marines DRM); `4992473` tacking crew, crew disorganized and signal halyards (phase 2: 5.D.2a, hull #4, rigging #3); `c180183` the crew overlay (phase 3: crew plotted by hand); `4857292` fire and explosions (phase 4: 8.E, 9.B.1c–d, hull #10 and #12, struck ships fighting fires); `032a61e` repairs (phase 5: 8.F, 8.G.1b, 9.B.2); `62ecfcc` boarding data and marine fire (boarding spec B1–B2: 7.G); `dc94cb1` grappling and ungrappling (B3: 8.C, 8.A.6 grappled groups, 5.D.5, 9.C.4); `c627e52` boarders, boarding combat and capture (B4: 10.A–10.E, 10.G.2–9, 10.H.1–2, 4.B.1, 8.C.5, 8.D.4, VI.B.2–3); `35463b9` prizes (B5: 10.G.5–7, 10.H.2–4, 1.G.8); `b6ad9e4` multi-ship boarding (B6: 10.F.1–3, the sixth-ship rule) and the 10.G.5 original crew joining its own side's attackers (user decision 2026-11-15). The crew-tasks and boarding rows were all re-checked against `b6ad9e4` when both specs were finished (crew-tasks spec phase 6). The rule-fixes sub-project (2026-11-16): `809d4df` sinking and sail-check rows closed, 6.C.3 recorded as deliberate; `a47139c` a stopped ship that turns first moves no further (5.E.8); `de64f03` reverse tack +3 (5.D.3b); `fe58e30` not in irons the g-t after a calm (3.D.4); `88d3ed7` same-segment bear-offs ordered by d10 (6.D.3c); `3398326` friendly ships make blocking hexes (7.B.10–12); `d125e2e` voluntary MGN reduction (7.C.14); `8f2c687` game over when a side cannot move or fight (2.A.1); `7d80d7e` prizes grapple, ungrapple and unfoul (10.H.2); `7a5c9dc` exploded ships removed at end of turn (8.G.1f). The sea-state sub-project (2026-11-16, `feature/sea-state` at `4263e1c`, commits `8efcfce` sea state 0-5 and a heavy-weather scenario, `c4f3b87` lower-deck guns shut by sea state (7.F.2), then the view-only sea model, swell, ship motion and glide playback) was checked against that head.

**Purpose:** a feature-level list of what the board game has that Leeward lacks, and of the places where both have a feature but the outcome differs significantly. It is not a play-by-play comparison. UI and presentation differences are ignored.

**Method:** eight reviewers each read one slice of the rules and compared it with the code. The lead reviewer then re-read the code behind every finding marked ✔ below. Unmarked findings come from the slice reviews only, and should be treated as reliable but not re-checked. Findings marked **?** could not be settled from the rules text alone, usually because the deciding chart or diagram is an image.

**Legend:**
- **Known**: already listed in `CLAUDE.md` as Milestone 2/3 or as a known limitation.
- **New**: not recorded anywhere in the project docs.
- Status words, the same in §1 (Type column) and §3 (lead word of the Leeward column): **Missing** (Leeward lacks it), **Differs** (both have it, the outcome differs), **Partly fixed** (a difference was fixed, another remains), **Fixed** (it now matches the rules). A §3 row with no lead word still differs.

## 4. Deliberate simplifications (for the record)

These are conscious design choices. They are listed because each one removes board-game outcomes.

- **Ships are 2-hex (Phase 1 done).** Per-hex LOS and partial targets (7.B.4–6), range to the closest hex, rake per 7.E.2, the automatic half broadside, bow-pivot turning and speed counting turns (5.E.2) are implemented. **Phase 2 done:** temporary overlap and rollback for swinging sterns (6.B), stern-vs-stern contests (6.C.5) and partial drift of a single unfouled ship (8.A.5: the free end drifts, the ship pivots, then fouling is rolled; groups never partially drift). **Phase 3 done:** explicit battery orders (7.B.1): full broadside, bow or stern battery (half broadside −8), split sides at partial targets, chosen in the fire panel.
- **A separate fire-declaration step after movement** (2.B.2, 7.A.2). Orders stay hidden until both sides have submitted. The outcome is the same as the board game's simultaneous fire.
- **Two sides, one human against the stub AI.** The multi-player rules (signalling 11.B, limited information 14.C) have nothing to apply to.
- **Struck ships cannot be targeted** (see the 9.C.3 row in §3).
- **Game over when a side cannot move OR fight** (2.A.1, user decision 2026-11-16). The rule says "move and fight"; Leeward ends the game only when a side has no ship that can do either, so a dismasted ship with guns left keeps its side in (`victory_controller.gd` (`can_move_or_fight`)).
- **Contested hex: the winner always enters** (6.C.3, user decision 2026-11-16). The rules let the winner of a contested hex choose to force the loser into it; Leeward never does (`movement_resolver.gd` (`_stern_contest`, `_resolve_contested_hex`)). Same behaviour as the 6.B.5 stern contest (the winner turns itself).
- **Grappling and ungrappling are ordered in a prompt** (4.A.1, SoP V.C–D; boarding spec §8 Q1). STATUS_ADJUSTMENT pauses for the player's grapple orders, rolls them, then pauses for the ungrapple orders, each only when the player has a choice; the AI decides at once. Same outcome as the board game's sub-phases.
- **Automatic crew allocation** (4.B). The player may leave section-by-section plotting to fixed rules (`CrewAuto`, crew-tasks spec §3.3; default on, always on for the AI; the player turns it off per ship in the crew overlay): every fire gets a section first (8.E.2, see §3), tasks in progress continue, boarders follow the ship's boarding order, an ordered sail change takes the marines first (unless a fire, a fixable critical effect, fouling or an adjacent enemy holds them back), then S4, S3, S2, S1. Repairs (rule A5, RS then RR then RH) take the marines when they are free, and a sailor section only when no enemy ship is within 15 hexes, so Automatic never gives up gunfire in action for a repair. The result is always a legal plot.

- **Sea state 0 (calm)** (extension, sea-state design A1). The sea scale runs 0–5; 0 is not in the rules and has no effect on gunnery (lower-deck guns are never shut, no dismasted modifier). It exists for the look of the water and for scenarios that want a flat sea.

## 5. Implemented and matching

Implemented with essentially the same outcome as the rules:

- Movement allowance lookup with rigging-level caps (5.A.2, 5.F.2–3).
- Sea state 1–5 (3.B.5) and lower-deck guns (7.F.2, 7.C.12e): the sea against the ship's freeboard shuts her lower-deck guns, the LDGM (`lower_deck_modifier`, modifier `lower_deck_closed`) taken off the BGN of the leeward broadside when the sea equals her freeboard (none at WF=L or WF=R) and off all gunfire when it is higher. The leeward side is `FireGeometry.leeward_side`; the rule is the pure `FireRules.lower_deck_closed` used by `GunneryCalculator.preview`, so the resolver, the AI and the fire panel agree; the selected ship card's Guns rows read the same rule and mark a shut broadside ("lower deck closed · sea 4") before firing. The dismasted-firer −sea state modifier (7.C.12e) uses the same scale.
- No MA gained by turning into a faster WF (5.B.3).
- A stopped ship which uses its first MP to turn can move no further that g-t (5.E.8). `MovementValidator._build_plotting_state` ends the plot after such a turn, as for a turn at MA 0.
- Blocked wind: MA −1 (never to 0) when every windward hex (two at WF=C/B, one at WF=R) is held by a ship at FS or above that is not dismasted (5.G.1–3). Checked at the plotted position against the other ships where they stand. `BlockedWind` (`blocked_wind.gd`), used by `ShipStateController.get_movement_allowance` and the movement validator.
- Sail-change cost, timing and cancellation when a crew section is lost (5.F.6–9, 4.A.3b, 4.B.2–3), worked by any section, marines included (4.B.1); its sailor sections do not serve the guns (4.B.4).
- No more than two turns per g-t, pivoting on the bow (5.C.1–2).
- A turn into WF=L stops the ship (5.B.4).
- Reverse tack (5.D.3b): a ship at WF=L that turns back the way it turned in takes the Reversing Tack DRM, +3. `ShipState.irons_entry_turn` records the turn that brought the ship to WF=L (cleared when it leaves WF=L; a wind shift into WF=L sets none, so no +3), and `MovementResolver` (`is_reverse_tack`, `_calculate_tacking_drm`, `apply_results`) adds the +3 to the Tacking roll and to the planning chance. The planner's calls: the +3 applies whenever the ship is still at WF=L and turns the other way (the rules' "just turned" is not limited to the previous g-t), and it stacks with the in-irons +1.
- Simultaneous segment-by-segment movement (6.A.1–2).
- Contested-hex d6 roll with ties and the CQ and speed DRMs (6.C.2a–b, 6.C.6).
- Bearing off as the owner's decision: legal turns only, chart odds and plotted-MP DRM, turn then deceleration minimum, once per g-t; the player is asked mid-playback with the other ships' plots shown; the AI never rams (6.D.1–6.D.5).
- Order of bear-offs on one segment (6.D.3c): earlier segments come first (6.D.3b); potential collisions found on the same segment are decided once its moves are made, and two or more each roll a d10 from the resolver's RNG, highest first, ties re-rolled among the tied (planner's call), which decides who is asked first and the order of the dice; the order is logged (`BEAR_OFF_ORDER`, in the combat log) and repeats when the resolution is re-run after an answer (`movement_resolver.gd` (`_potential_collision`, `_resolve_pending_bear_offs`, `_order_bear_offs`)). Readings: a situation that arises only because another ship stopped (it sails into the stern that ship kept) is decided right after that one, with no roll; a head-on pair whose blocker has not yet made its step is decided at once, as before. A successful bear-off still replays the resolution, so a later situation it removes disappears.
- Both ships stop on a collision, fouling at 6+, dismasted ships never foul (6.E.2, 6.E.4–5).
- Collision rigging loss: d6−1/d6+1/2d6 by sail state, for the ramming ship, or both ships head-on (6.E.3).
- The gunnery pipeline: TGF → BGN with French guns (by the navy using the ship, 1.G.8), CQ, sections firing, guns dismounted, initial broadside, rake bonus, plain sail at WS 2 or more, WS 4, dismantling shot, FGN = MGN + d6, slashed results, damage columns by sail state with furled sails halved, lowest section first, crew split by cause, stern rake doubling crew hits (7.C–7.E).
- Simultaneous fire with stern rakes, then bow rakes, then everything else in order (7.C.2).
